Step 04 — Consent

Consent, asked for properly

Three purposes, stated one by one. None pre-ticked.

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given for a stated purpose, and as easy to withdraw as it was to give. Most products bundle everything into one switch and call it consent. This screen does not.

The third purpose — keeping the policy on file — is genuinely optional. Decline it and everything that follows still works.

Step 10 — The hospital

Where you are admitted is a coverage decision

Same procedure, same policy, four different settlements.

The room-rent cap is a percentage of the sum insured, not of the bill — so the cap is fixed while the room rate is not. On a ₹5,00,000 policy capped at 1%, a ₹5,000 room is eligible in full and nothing is deducted. A ₹12,000 room makes 58% of every associated medical expense yours as well, because the same ratio is applied to them. The hospital is the last variable still open to you at the admission desk, which makes it the one worth showing first.

Choose one below. The rate travels with you into the estimate, the verdict and the settlement — every figure on the screens after this is computed from whatever you pick here.

What this screen is not

This is a walkthrough of how we intend the product to work, and this screen is the thinnest part of it. There is no live search behind the list. The four hospitals are invented and so are their tariffs; none of it has been near a real network file.

The built product would read the insurer's empanelment list and the hospital's published rate card, then rank what is reachable by what it would actually cost you. The demo shows the shape of that decision with numbers we control, so every rupee downstream can be checked by hand.

Before the form is signed

Step 12 — The verdict

The room-rent trap, made visible

Most Indian policies cap the eligible room at a percentage of the sum insured per day — commonly 1%.

Bill a room above the cap and the insurer does not just trim the room charge. It reduces the room and a defined class of associated medical expenses by the same ratio. IRDAI's 2020 standards carve four heads out of that deduction — pharmacy, implants, diagnostics and ICU. Because the whole thing is arithmetic, it can be computed exactly, in advance.

Break it yourself. Drag the room rate and watch the phone recompute.

This policy has no room-rent cap. The clause does not apply, so the slider is switched off.

Step 14 — Through the stay

Checked, then re-checked

One pre-admission estimate is wrong the moment anything changes.

So coverage is re-checked rather than checked. Four checkpoints re-run the engine on whichever state changed — the ward actually offered, the diagnostics actually ordered, the implant actually used, the stay actually lengthened. And the deduction applies per day, at the rate actually billed, so a mid-stay change is computed by segment rather than retroactively across the whole stay.

Step 15 — Settlement

Close honestly

What the prototype is, and what it is not.

Synthetic policies only — three archetypes, modelled for this demo. No live NHCX sandbox connection, no clinical validation, and no measured outcomes. Every impact number this project quotes for itself is a proposed pilot target, not a result: the metric would be the share of tracked admissions whose out-of-pocket at settlement matches the pre-admission estimate within 5%, measured against a baseline cohort during the pilot.

The national context, with sources. Of roughly ₹1.2 lakh crore of health claims filed in FY24, 71.3% was paid; ₹15,100 crore was disallowed at settlement and ₹10,937 crore repudiated (IRDAI Annual Report 2023-24). Out-of-pocket spending remains 39.4% of total health expenditure, down from 64.2% in 2013-14 (National Health Accounts 2021-22). The disallowance figure, not the repudiation figure, is the one families meet as a surprise: the claim was approved, and the money still did not arrive.
